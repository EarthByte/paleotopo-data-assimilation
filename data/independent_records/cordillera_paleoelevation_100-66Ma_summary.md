# North American Cordillera, 100–66 Ma: independent paleoelevation records

Quantitative Late Cretaceous paleoaltimetry of the Cordillera rests on a handful of isotope studies, framed by a well-dated set of marine datums. The strongest constraint is Snell et al. (2014, EPSL), whose clumped-isotope temperature contrast between Sheep Pass lake carbonates in east-central Nevada and North Horn paleosols in central Utah places the Nevadaplano 2.2–3.1 km above the Utah foreland, and at least 2 km once age and climate uncertainty are included, in the Maastrichtian. Foreman et al. (2011) infer a hypsometric mean of about 2.6 km for the Montana Sevier belt from Campanian foreland δ18O gradients, without a stated error. The main debate is the elevation of the Nevadaplano itself: Lechler et al. (2013) obtain at most 2 km for the same Sheep Pass basin, but they compare against an Eocene coastal reference, whereas Snell et al. correct for secular cooling between coeval sites. Mid-Cretaceous Newark Canyon carbonates (Fetrow et al., preprint) indicate low hinterland elevation around 113–98 Ma but give no number, and Dettman & Lohmann (2000) argue for high-altitude snowmelt in Laramide rivers only qualitatively; neither is tabulated. Mulch et al. (2006), Cassel et al., Mix et al. (2011), Chamberlain et al. (2012), Fan's Laramide basins and Wolfe et al. (1998) constrain Paleogene time only. Six marine datums fix sea level along the Western Interior Seaway margin (Tropic Shale, Book Cliffs, Lewis Shale, Bearpaw), in the Great Valley forearc and in the Nanaimo Group. The Snell row and the dated marine datums (Lewis, Bearpaw) are the most reliable.
