# Central Pangean Mountains, 330–270 Ma: independent paleoelevation records

Quantitative paleoaltimetry of the Variscan–Appalachian–Mauritanide belt is almost nonexistent, and that is the result. One peer-reviewed paleoaltimeter exists: Dusséaux et al. (2021, EPSL) use hydrogen isotopes of meteoric water recorded in synkinematic muscovite from Massif Central detachment zones (about 318–310 Ma). A δD contrast of roughly 76‰ against a foreland basin yields 3.4 ± 0.7 km, which the authors treat as a minimum. The same group's thesis work gives at least 2.5 ± 0.9 km for the Armorican Massif internal zones (about 319–303 Ma), but that value is unpublished. Both estimates rest on a foreland reference that is younger (about 295 Ma) and assumed to lie near sea level. No clumped-isotope, paleobotanical or basalt-vesicularity paleoelevations were found for the belt. Becq-Giraudon et al. (1996) describe high-altitude phenomena in the Massif Central, and Pfeifer et al. (2021) infer cold Permian uplands from Lodève loess, but neither yields a number that could be read and checked, so both stay out of the table. For the Appalachians the only figure is Slingerland & Furlong's (1989) model-based 3.5–4.5 km of Early Permian relief, which is a tectonic estimate rather than a paleoaltimeter. The Ouachita–Marathon and Mauritanide segments have no quantitative estimate. Sea level around the belt is fixed by marine datums in the Appalachian foreland (Ames Limestone), the Cantabrian foreland (Sierra del Cuera), the Carnic Alps (Auernig Group) and the Marathon foreland (Permian Basin). The Dusséaux 2021 row and the marine datums are the reliable entries.
